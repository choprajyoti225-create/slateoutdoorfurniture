Get mildew out of outdoor cushions (and the foam underneath)

Black speckling on the cover is the symptom. If the foam core is still damp you will be doing this again in a fortnight.

Time
An afternoon
Best season
Summer
Reading
2 min
Written
11 April 2026

Outdoor cushion fabric — acrylic canvas, olefin, solution-dyed polyester — does not itself rot. Mildew on it is feeding on what has landed on it: pollen, sunscreen, spilled drinks, skin oils. That is good news, because it means the spores have nothing to grip once you remove the food.

Do this dry, first

Take the cushions somewhere dry and brush the speckling off with a soft brush before any water touches them. Wet mildew smears into the weave and sets; dry mildew mostly falls out. This step removes maybe 70% of the visible problem and takes two minutes.

Then oxygen bleach, never chlorine

Mix oxygen bleach (sodium percarbonate — sold as laundry booster) at about 60g per 4 litres of warm water. Sponge it on, leave it 15 minutes, agitate gently, rinse thoroughly with a hose.

Chlorine bleach will lift the stain faster and will also strip the fluorocarbon water repellent off the fabric and fade solution-dyed fibres unevenly. You get a clean cushion that soaks up water like a sponge from then on — a worse problem than the one you started with.

The part everyone skips: the foam

Squeeze the cushion hard at one corner. If any water comes out, or if it feels cool and heavy compared to a dry one, the foam core is holding water and the mildew will be back within two weeks regardless of how clean the cover is.

Unzip the cover, pull the foam, stand it on edge — on edge, not flat — on towels somewhere warm with air moving past it. Open-cell foam takes two to four days to dry properly, and the outside feels dry long before the centre is. A fan aimed at it halves that.

If the foam smells sour even when dry, it is done. Replacement outdoor cushion foam is reticulated (open-celled by design, so water passes straight through) and is worth the upgrade — it drains in hours rather than days.

Stopping the repeat

Cushions stored flat in a stack, or in a sealed plastic box, will grow mildew even when they went in dry, because the small amount of moisture in them has nowhere to go. Store them upright, loosely, in a breathable bag, somewhere with air movement. A cheap cotton laundry sack beats an expensive waterproof box every time.

Re-proof the covers with a fabric-safe fluorocarbon spray once a season. Water should bead and roll; when it starts to darken the fabric instead, the repellent has gone.

You will need

  • Soft brush
  • Oxygen bleach (sodium percarbonate)
  • Bucket
  • Old towels
