Fix a wobbly hardwood chair without taking it apart

The bolts on a folding or knock-down garden chair need tightening in a specific order, on the ground, with weight in the seat. Most wobbles are that.

Time
Ten minutes
Best season
Spring
Reading
2 min
Written
19 March 2026

A hardwood garden chair that rocks diagonally is almost never broken. Seasonal movement has loosened its fasteners, and because you tighten them with the chair in the air, you lock the frame into a racked shape.

Tighten it loaded, and in order

Put the chair on a flat, hard surface. Sit on it. Have someone else — or reach down yourself — snug every bolt in this order:

  1. All four seat-rail bolts, gently, just to contact.
  2. Back-leg to back-rail bolts, fully.
  3. Front-leg bolts, fully.
  4. Back to seat-rail bolts, fully.
  5. Return to the seat rails and take them fully tight.

Weight in the seat holds the frame in the geometry it will actually be used in. Two passes, snug-then-tight, lets the joints settle square instead of pulling to whichever bolt you happened to reach first.

Nine times out of ten the wobble is gone and you are done.

If the bolt spins without tightening

The threaded insert in the timber has stripped. Do not keep turning — you are chewing the hole out. Back the bolt out, fill the hole with exterior polyurethane glue and a tight-fitting hardwood dowel, let it cure overnight, then drill and re-tap. In teak, use a slow-set glue: the natural oil interferes with quick-grab adhesives and a five-minute epoxy will let go in one summer.

If it is a glued mortise and tenon

A traditional glued joint that has opened up cannot be fixed by tightening anything, and squirting glue into the gap does not work — there is no clean surface for it to bond to, and it will fail again in weeks.

The honest repair is to knock the joint apart, scrape both faces back to bare timber, re-glue with a waterproof PVA (D4) or polyurethane, and clamp for the full cure time. Two sash clamps, one overnight. It is a 45-minute job done properly and a recurring annoyance done badly.

The thing that actually causes it

Chairs that live on an uneven patio get racked every time someone sits down, and the fasteners walk loose within a season. If one chair in a set is always the wobbly one, the problem is the paving slab it stands on. Move it, or shim the short leg with a stick-on felt pad, and the repair holds.

You will need

  • Allen keys or socket set
  • Exterior-grade PVA or polyurethane glue
  • Two sash clamps (only for glued joints)
