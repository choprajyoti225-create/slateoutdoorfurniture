Aluminium or steel: decide by your postcode, not your budget

Within eight miles of the coast, aluminium is the only sensible answer. Inland, steel's extra weight is a real advantage.

Time
Ten minutes
Best season
Any time
Reading
2 min
Written
12 January 2026

Metal garden furniture comes in two materials at similar prices, and the marketing for both lists the same virtues. The decision is actually simple once you know two things about where you live: how salty the air is, and how windy.

Salt air

Steel rusts through; aluminium oxidises on the surface and then stops. Powder coating protects both, but coating always fails eventually at a chip or a weld, and what happens next is the whole difference.

On steel, a breach in a salt-laden atmosphere becomes a hole. Chloride ions break down the oxide layer continuously, so the corrosion never self-limits. On aluminium, a breach forms a thin white oxide crust and the metal underneath is then protected by it.

Within roughly eight miles of open coast, or anywhere you can taste salt on a windy day, buy aluminium. This is not close.

Wind

Aluminium's weakness is that it is light — around a third the density of steel. A stackable aluminium chair weighs 3–4kg and will cartwheel across a garden in a gale. Steel bistro chairs at 7–9kg mostly stay put.

If you are inland and exposed, steel's weight is a feature, and the maintenance burden is manageable: an autumn and a spring walk-round checking welds and feet.

The other differences, briefly

  • Heat. Both get hot in direct sun. Dark powder coat on either will burn bare legs at around 60°C on a south-facing patio. Light colours and a cushion solve it; the material does not.
  • Repairability. Steel can be welded by any local fabricator for very little. Aluminium needs TIG and someone who knows what they are doing, and cast aluminium often cannot be repaired at all. If a joint fails out of warranty, steel is cheaper to save.
  • Cast vs tube. Cast aluminium (the heavy ornate kind) is a different product: 15–20kg a chair, so the wind problem disappears, but it is brittle and a dropped chair can crack rather than bend.
  • Wrought iron, if you are offered it second-hand, is neither of these. It is beautiful, extremely heavy, and needs repainting every three or four years. Buy it because you want it, not because it is practical.

If you cannot decide

Aluminium frame, and spend the difference on making it heavier where it counts: a proper parasol base and a set of chairs that stack so you can put them away. That combination outlasts both.
