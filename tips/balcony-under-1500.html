Seating a balcony that is only 1.4m deep

Below about 1.6m of depth, two facing chairs stop working and the whole layout has to change. Four arrangements that do fit.

Time
An afternoon
Best season
Spring
Reading
2 min
Written
24 April 2026

The number that decides a small balcony layout is depth, not area. Two people sitting opposite each other need about 1.6m between the backs of their chairs before someone's knees are in the way. Most flats built since 2000 have balconies between 1.2m and 1.5m deep.

So on a shallow balcony, stop trying to make a bistro set work and change the geometry.

Measure the real depth first

Tape out the usable rectangle on the floor, subtracting:

  • the door swing, if it opens outward (often 700mm of unusable arc)
  • 100mm along the railing, which you will not want to sit pressed against
  • any radiator, drainage channel, or upstand

What remains is often 300mm less than the plan says. Work from the tape, not the number.

Four layouts that work under 1.6m

Both chairs facing out. Two chairs side by side against the building wall, facing the view, with a narrow side table between or beside them. This is the single best use of a shallow balcony and it is the one people try last. Nobody needs to face each other to have a conversation outdoors.

Bench along the long wall, stool opposite. A 1200mm bench takes almost no depth — a slim outdoor bench is 350–400mm — and a single light stool can be pulled into place and put away. Seats three when needed.

Rail-mounted table. A folding table that clamps to the railing gives you a surface with zero floor footprint. Check your lease: many buildings forbid anything fixed to a balustrade, and the clamp-on ones rated for it are specific about railing profile and maximum load.

One good chair. If the balcony is 1.2m deep and used by one person, a single generous lounge chair placed diagonally in a corner beats two cramped upright ones. It is the honest answer more often than people want to hear.

Weight matters more than you think

Balconies have a design load, typically 1.5–2.5 kN/m² for residential, and heavy planters eat into it fast — a 50cm terracotta pot of wet compost is around 40kg. On a cantilevered concrete balcony, keep the heavy things close to the building wall rather than out at the edge, where they load the slab at its greatest leverage.

Pick furniture that stacks or folds flat

On a shallow balcony you will move the furniture constantly — to open the door properly, to clean, to dry laundry. Anything that does not fold or stack becomes an obstacle within a month. Look for chairs that stack at least three high and fold to under 100mm.

You will need

  • Tape measure
  • Chalk or masking tape
