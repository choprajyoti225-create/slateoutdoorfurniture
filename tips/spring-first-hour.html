The first hour of spring: a walk-round in order

One pass, one bucket, no shopping trip. What to look at on each material and in what sequence, so nothing waits on something else.

Time
An afternoon
Best season
Spring
Reading
2 min
Written
1 March 2026

Do this on the first dry, mild day — ideally before you want to use the furniture, because half the jobs need something to cure or dry. The sequence matters: everything that needs drying time starts first.

1. Uncover and separate (10 min)

Pull the covers off, take everything off the wall, stand it where it will get air. Hang the covers over a line — they have been damp for four months and they will be mouldy on the inside seam whatever the packaging claimed.

2. Start the cushion foam drying (5 min)

If cushions were stored outside at all, unzip one and check the foam for cool dampness. If it is damp, get all the foam out and standing on edge now, because it needs two to four days and nothing else on this list does.

3. Wash everything (25 min)

Warm water, stiff nylon brush, along the grain on timber. Do not reach for cleaners yet — most "stains" are last autumn's tannin film and come off with plain water. No pressure washer on wood or on powder coat.

While you wash, you are inspecting. This is the real purpose of the step.

4. Inspect in this order, writing down what you find

  • Feet. Every leg: is the plastic foot present? Missing feet are why legs rust from inside.
  • Welds and leg bottoms on metal: fingernail along each one, feeling for a catch.
  • End grain on timber: crumbly fibre means rot has started.
  • Bolts. Not tightened yet — just noted if any are rusted, missing, or spinning free.
  • Drain holes in tube frames and table rims: clear them with a wire.
  • Rattan strands: slack ones are fine, cracked ones are terminal.
  • Glass tops: look at the edges for chips; a chipped edge is where a top eventually shatters.

5. Tighten, loaded (15 min)

Now go round the bolts, with the chair on the ground and weight in the seat, snug-then-tight. This is where most wobbles disappear.

6. One shopping list, once

You now know exactly what you need — usually plastic feet, one tube of touch-up enamel, and maybe a bag of oxygen bleach. Ordering once, after inspecting everything, is the difference between this being a one-hour job and a three-weekend job.

7. Leave the finishing until last

Sealer, oil, fabric proofer: all of these want a clean, fully dry surface, and "dry" after a wash means 24–48 hours for timber, not an afternoon. Put them in next weekend, and do not let that delay you using the furniture in the meantime.

You will need

  • Bucket of warm water
  • Stiff nylon brush
  • Allen keys
  • Notebook
