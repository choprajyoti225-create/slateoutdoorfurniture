Catch rust on powder-coated steel in the two weeks that matter

A powder coat fails at a single chip and then lifts outward. There is a short window where a cotton bud of touch-up paint saves the frame.

Time
Ten minutes
Best season
Autumn
Reading
2 min
Written
29 January 2026

Powder coating does not wear thin the way paint does. It stays perfect until something breaches it — a stone chip, a dragged foot, a screw overtightened at the factory — and then water gets underneath and the coating lifts off in a growing island around the breach.

The tell is a raised blister with a rust-orange halo, usually on a weld seam or the bottom 100mm of a leg. Those are the two places where coating goes on thinnest.

The window

From first blister to a coin-sized lifted patch is about two weeks of wet weather. Inside that window the repair is genuinely trivial. After it, you are grinding back to bare metal over an area you cannot colour-match, and the frame starts to look repaired.

So the real tip is scheduling, not technique: walk your metal furniture once in late autumn and once in early spring, and run a fingernail along every weld and every leg bottom. You are feeling for a catch, not looking for a colour.

The repair

  1. Pick off the loose coating with a fingernail. Anything that lifts was already gone — if you leave it, you are sealing water in.
  2. Sand the exposed steel with 220-grit, wet. Take it back to grey metal, feathering the coating edge so there is no ledge.
  3. Dab on rust converter and wait the full stated time, usually 20–30 minutes. It turns the remaining oxide into an inert black layer. Skipping this is why touch-ups fail in a year.
  4. Build the enamel up in two or three thin coats with a cotton bud, not a brush. Thin coats dome slightly and blend; one thick coat leaves a visible bead.

Feet are the real problem

Steel legs sitting directly on a wet patio wick water upward into the tube and rust from the inside, which you will never see until the leg folds. Check that every leg has its plastic foot. They cost pennies and they fall off constantly. If a foot is missing, a blob of exterior silicone in the tube end is an acceptable field repair — but keep the tube's drain hole clear if it has one.

Aluminium

If the frame is aluminium, the same white-crusted blistering can happen, but it is oxide, not rust, and it does not propagate the same way. Scrub with a nylon pad, rinse, and touch up whenever convenient. Aluminium furniture does not have a two-week window; steel does.

You will need

  • Cotton buds
  • 220-grit wet-and-dry paper
  • Rust converter
  • Matched touch-up enamel
